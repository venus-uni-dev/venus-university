# Venus University — Design Guide

## 0. How to read this guide

- This is the jumping-off point for anybody, human or agent, changing this repository: what the game is, where things live, and the rules a change has to keep. Read it before the code, and read `UI_STYLE_GUIDE.md` as well for anything that shows on screen.
- The code is authoritative. Where this guide and the code disagree, the code is right and the guide is stale: fix the guide.
- Present tense, nothing aspirational: a feature not in the code does not exist, whatever is written here.
- Appearance — shape, colour, type, layout, motion and the class names that carry them — is `UI_STYLE_GUIDE.md`'s and is never restated here.
- Hand-authored and disk-derived inventories are not listed here: backgrounds, poses, tag vocabularies, the class and shop catalogs, the lorebook, the bots' scripts. Only the rules they follow are.

## 1. What the game is

- A local, single-player, AI-driven dating sim in visual-novel form: an Electron desktop app for Windows with an NVIDIA or an AMD (RDNA 3 or 4) GPU, and a browser build of the same renderer that itch.io hosts. React 19, TypeScript (strict), Zustand, Motion (`motion/react`), Vitest, plain CSS. No server, no accounts, no env vars — everything the player configures lives in `data/settings.json` on the desktop and in the browser's own storage on the web.
- **The writer is a cloud LLM.** By default it is Google Gemini over the native API; the player may instead name any endpoint that speaks OpenAI chat completions, with its own key, model id and reasoning effort. It invents characters, writes every scene as structured JSON the app plays back VN-style, answers two cheap classifiers, and keeps the books between scenes. The persona it writes as is RITA. The cloud pictures — rooms, the graduation picture, the phone's photos — are Gemini's image models on the Gemini key under Gemini; under a custom endpoint they go to its own images endpoint, Google's by default or anything speaking OpenRouter's Images API, on the one model the player names for every picture but a photo, which may be drawn on any model that endpoint offers. Either way they are skipped without a key.
- **The artist is a locally managed ComfyUI**, installed under `data/services` by the app itself. It is optional: creating characters needs it, playing does not. The browser build has no ComfyUI at all: every control that needs it is dead there under one notice, and the cloud renders — rooms, the graduation picture — still run.
- **The premise is fixed**: the reader starts as a freshman at Venus University in the city of Veridan, and a playthrough runs a semester at a time, spring and fall alternating from his first spring, until he graduates at his senior fall's ceremony. A spring runs from January 19 (a Monday, day 0) to Friday May 22, a fall from Monday August 17 to Friday December 18, on the same day numbers. Two slots a day, day and night; a slot holds at most one scene.
- **The end is reachable, not winnable.** Every semester's graduation morning opens an authored epilogue of goodbyes, and the ending after it offers the next semester, except after his own graduation. Two things end a playthrough early: debt past a floor, and being caught at something expellable.
- **Content**: scenes may be explicit. Two settings shape it — `noNsfwImages` withholds the nude wardrobe and the CGs, `lessNsfwText` softens what RITA is asked for. Neither is a filter, and the notes beside them are the whole of what the app promises.

### Vocabulary that is load-bearing

- **The reader** is the protagonist in the fiction ("he"); **the player** is the person at the keyboard. Prompts and copy say "the reader".
- **Memories** are what one character recalls about the reader. **History** is the playthrough log of finished-scene summaries. The **transcript** is the scene as delivered — the reader's action lines and every reply line — and the writer is shown its tail; the **scene log** is everything the player has read. None of them is "chat history".
- A **playthrough** is one continuous game: a folder of saves plus the one **record** (`playthrough.json`) they are all read against; before the record exists the folder holds one **enrollment** instead, the semester waiting on its timetable. A **semester** is one term of it: the record holds the first at its top level and every later one under its own id beside the one it continued from, and a save names the one it is in. A later semester's enrollment is its **registration**, kept beside the record until its timetable is picked. Load Game calls a playthrough by the name the player gives it from inside it, and by `Playthrough N`, its place in creation order, until then or once the name is blanked. A **save** is a file in it — a slot-save at a boundary, one **autosave**, the last decision point reached, or one of ninety **manual saves** the player writes from the menu.
- An **event** is a plan the reader agreed to with somebody. An **occasion** happens whether he turns up or not, belongs to nobody, and is the only one of the two that can close the university.
- **charId** is the on-disk identity (a UUID). **charKey** (`first_last`) is the prompt identity — the only form the model sees or emits — translated at the edge and never used in a path. Neither is the display name.
- The **roster** is the twelve girls on campus in a semester, picked at New Game or on a later semester's New Game+ screen and never fewer; a **graduate** has left it at a graduation and stays loaded and textable, gone from Veridan; the **cast** is who is in the scene. A **slot** is one half of one day; the **boundary** is the moment a slot ends and the next begins.
- The **shipped cast** lives under `assets/characters` and is read-only; the **player's own** characters live under `data/characters`.

## 2. Design and mechanics

**Time.** Each season has its own fixed calendar in code — holidays, add/drop day, midterms and finals weeks, a break week (spring break, the fall's Thanksgiving break), the vacation after finals, the graduation ceremony; an occasion both share means the same thing in both, and add/drop, finals and graduation fall on the same day numbers. Every date that names a month is formatted in its own semester's season off a year with no leap day, so a date carried back across several breaks still prints its own month and day; a weekday is read off the day number alone, both day 0s being Mondays. Each semester invents a handful more when it is generated (a gala, a dance, a career fair, a concert, weekly fillers, and fillers on the break week's days no fixed occasion holds); a generated occasion can never cancel a class. An occasion announces itself ahead in the prompts — a half-day the slot before, a day the day before, a run as many days before as it lasts — except those with a lead-up of their own. Weekends hold no classes. Spring break sends the half of the roster the reader likes most off campus for the week, never with him; the Thanksgiving break keeps everybody on campus under the break week's other rules, and the vacation after finals keeps everybody on campus until graduation. The leavers are picked off the affection the ending before the Monday of midterms week projects, or off that slot's own where no scene precedes it, and banked with that slot's opening for the boundary to assign; the opening writes each leaver who has given him her number, has not blocked him and is friendly or warmer a few texts telling him she is going.

**Weather.** The whole semester's sky is rolled once as the semester is written and kept on its semester of the record, one reading per slot: clear, rain or a thunderstorm. Rain tends to keep falling for a few half-days, a storm mostly grows out of rain and then clears the air, and storms lean toward the evening and grow commoner as a spring runs on and rarer as a fall does; the authored first day, graduation, the Selkie MusicFest and the holidays held outdoors are clear whatever the roll said, and the epilogue's night is always clear. The prompts are told what the sky is doing and for how long it has been doing it. A wet slot draws a background's rain render where it has one and the dry one otherwise, plays the rain or storm loop in place of the room tone — heard indoors through a window, so a silent room is not silent under rain, and the landing, where he is indoors on his phone, hears the same window — and wears the weather's mark where the sun or the moon would be: on the splash, the scene stamp, the landing and the calendar, which also forecasts the week ahead.

**Semesters.** The ending's Continue carries the playthrough into the next semester; continuing again from an older ending's Goodbyes save makes a sibling semester beside the first, and nothing is ever deleted. Two intakes move the roster on: the reader and the girls who arrived in a spring move up a year at the end of each fall, those who arrived in a fall at the end of each spring — in the first semester a freshman counts as a spring arrival and anybody older as a fall one — and a senior whose intake moves up graduates instead; a fall's ceremony also graduates one senior of the other intake, picked when the fall begins. Who graduates is one rule that the ceremony's names, the goodbyes, the last posts, her own cast line and the carry all read. Continue builds the carry off the ending's own state: every date the next semester keeps — memories, posts, chats, dating and break-up dates, the pairs made friends — is moved onto its clock, and a memory carried across a break does not age over it, so every girl opens the semester as she stood on graduation day; money, stats, tallies, inventory, flags, notes, the pairs' affinities and the conversations go on, each graduate's last phone impression folded into her memories; the timetable, class records, history, replays and plans go onto the record as the semester's log, and the grades, aced exams and attendance onto the transcript; the reader's job, the girls' jobs, the overlay, the outings, the epilogue's flags and every occasion turned down are dropped. New Game+ opens on the continuing girls, locked, the graduates kept off it, and the player fills the rest; Start asks the writer for a catalog around the returning girls' majors that runs no class an earlier semester of the line ran, the newcomers' profiles, the returning girls' posts from the break and before a spring their spring break plans, and each graduate's life since. The registration is written before the registrar opens, so leaving it loses nothing: Continue and Load Game put him back on it, and Back on New Game+ asks first, the Goodbyes save being where Continue is offered again. Finalize deals the roster's weeks and shifts — a returning girl keeps her employer, her haunts and how many classes she takes — dates the break's posts after the last ending's posts, and writes the semester and its first save. A later semester's first morning is an ordinary slot opening told he is back on campus, with no arrival scroll, orientation scene or first night. The calendar pages back through every semester of the save's own line, each month in its own semester's dates and every day of an earlier one past, read off that semester's log and catalog. Each semester's ending draws its own graduation picture.

**Graduates.** A graduate is in nothing that reads the roster — casting, schedules, the map, the overlay, the rolls, the farewells — so the classifier never names her and no plan can; an opening naming her is refused, a continuation may talk about her, and a meet-up agreed with her by text is refused as she is away. She keeps her thread, her posts, her page and her place on the Friends tab, out of town; what texting leaves her is kept among her memories rather than as the phone's one impression, her romance is never counted toward settling and no crush is rolled for her, but she still minds him dating somebody new, as her friends do, and breaks up with him by text. Every prompt that describes her says she graduated, what she majored in and what she is doing now. A graduate whose character is deleted is left out of the game, her save entry kept as it is.

**Backgrounds.** Beside the shipped places the player may bring backgrounds of their own from Advanced Settings, kept for every playthrough: a name, a day and a night picture and either half's rain render if wanted, whether the place is indoors, and a song it plays in both halves of the day. The name is the word the writer is offered, listed under its kind among the shipped ones and accepted from a reply like them; it is lowercased with every space an underscore, holds nothing but letters, digits and those underscores, and is refused where a shipped background, another of the player's or any character's room already answers to it. Each picture is cut to its centred 16:9 the moment it is picked, shrunk to 1920×1080 where larger, and written as PNG. A shipped name keeps its place over one of the player's brought under it from another build, and any listed background's name over a character's room id, on the stage, in the prompt and in the picker alike. A record naming a song this build lacks reads as songless rather than costing the place. One may be deleted at any time, mid-scene included: whatever named it falls back to the dorm, as a renamed character's room does.

**Characters.** A character is written by the LLM from a short brief: one woven personality paragraph, five behavior strings (one per relationship stage), a backstory, a four-field love life, likes and dislikes, gift preferences, a closed set of trait switches, the one reader stat she goes for, the pitch of her voice, which the player retunes, booru appearance tags that open on the two subject tags every render needs, hers to edit like the rest and left out of the writer's prompts, one pose, and a seed. Every image she has but her room comes from ComfyUI under that seed: the expression sprites, and optionally the alternate wardrobes and the CGs; her optional day/night room is a cloud render. Her portrait is cut from the neutral sprite, which always looks at the viewer whatever eye tag she was written with. She can also leave as a SillyTavern card — her neutral sprite framed chest-up from her detected face over the quad by day, her sheet written into the PNG's text chunks as V2 and V3 card JSON — and as a sprite pack of her seven default expressions under the Expressions extension's names, each copied again under the extension's other default labels that read closest to it, so every one of its 28 defaults finds a sprite. A regenerate opens on the prompt it is about to send — every tag as an editable list and the seed it would run under, a new random one by default; one expression is re-rendered on its own by clicking its sprite, as one CG is by clicking it in the gallery. Either room may instead be a picture the player uploads by clicking it in its gallery, on the Edit modal only and never while her room set renders: cut to its centred 16:9, shrunk to 1920×1080 where larger, and written as PNG over that variant; the night is not re-lit from an uploaded day. Each of those buttons keeps every group it last sent, whole, on her record and opens on them rather than on her tags from then on, so an edit to her tags reaches it only through the modal's reset, which puts back the groups her record would send and writes nothing until the render goes; the seed is never kept. A fill, and a button that has sent nothing, render from her tags. Which images exist is read off disk, never recorded, and a partial set counts as absent. Beside the three stock wardrobes she has five custom slots, each a wardrobe rendered from tags the player types and named by him: the slot's Outfit group *is* her record, written before every confirmed render and read back from there when its modal opens, whatever its button kept; a slot is emptied images first and record second, what its button and its sprites' buttons kept going with it. A slot's name is lowercased with every space taken out as it is written, and shown that way wherever it is shown, an older record's included. A slot may also carry instructions — when to wear it, completing "Use the <name> outfit when", the hint naming the outfit as RITA will read it — written on its first Generate and from its column afterwards. A slot rendered whole with instructions is offered to RITA: its tags and its instructions in her cast entry, and its name as the suffix she is written into it with, translated to the slot id at the edge as a charKey is, so a save only ever holds the slot id and a rename costs nothing. A name that is blank, is a set id or is already another of her offered outfits' is offered under the slot id instead. One without instructions reaches the stage only by the Cast modal's lock, and the missing-content sweep never invents one. A save holding a custom set that is no longer whole on disk draws her default wardrobe. Beside her eight stock CGs she has four custom CG slots, each a pair rendered as one set under one seed: a main image from position tags the player types, and its `_after` from the same groups with `after_sex` added, her aroused face in the main and her happy one in the `_after`, as the stock afterglows wear; the pair's tag form edits the main's face alone. A pair is named, instructed and emptied as a custom wardrobe slot is — the same name rule, its Position group being her record, images first and record second, its instructions a clause from "she" ("she is riding him") rather than the end of a sentence — and also carries the voice loop and the act loop its main image plays, its `_after` keeping the stock afterglow's slow breath and no act. The pair's Regenerate renders both images again; its `_after` may also be re-rolled alone. Its images sit in a folder of their own per slot, never beside the stock eight, whose regenerate replaces their folder whole. A pair whole on disk with instructions is offered to RITA while she is the only girl on screen — in the JSON rules after the stock acts, its instructions as its gloss, under its name for the main and the name with `_after` for its aftermath, translated to the slot id at the edge — and a name that is blank, is a CG id, or would collide either way with another of her offered pairs is offered under the slot id. A save holding a custom pair no longer whole on disk draws her as the sprite that CG retires to. Traits are switches the code branches on and are never written into a prompt. The brief she was written from, reference picture included, sits on her record and beside it in her folder until her sheet lands, so a write that failed or was interrupted is resumed from her card in one click; a card whose sheet was never written opens nothing. The New Character modal's Skip LLM filling box writes her sheet on the spot instead — her names, the description as her personality, the first pose in the manifest, one stock tag set per expression, `body` as her stat and nothing else — with no brief kept and nothing rendered, so her card opens at once on an empty sheet the player fills and renders by hand. Her pose, like any character's, is picked under Generation settings.

**The slot cycle.** A slot opens on a narration call (the first day is authored) behind a curtain, then the landing: the date, money, stats, the hour's commitments as buttons, topped up with the reader's lowest stats, and a text box. The first action is classified — who is named, class or work or project, public or private, where — the cast is drawn, and the scene call streams lines the player clicks through. Further actions are continuations on a rolling summary until RITA ends the scene. The ending fires its calls in parallel and writes one save when all the replies are in, none when the boundary ahead loses the game; the boundary applies the ledger, moves the clock and writes the slot-save. Everything the ending computes is projected and banked; the boundary assigns and never rolls, so a replayed boundary lands the same values. Once the boundary's curtain is down and before it says anything, the player is asked what each girl in the cast should remember of the scene — the ledger's memories of her, each to reword, retype or blank, and a blank row where it gave her none — and the answer is filed before the clock moves, so the slot-save carries it and a reload before it asks again; a boundary that ends the game, or one with nobody cast, asks nothing. While a reply is pending, still streaming or parked on the scene's ending, the player may interject: the unread lines are deleted, the departure clocks are charged as at any decision point, and an autosave is written before the continuation call goes out. An interjection taken from a rewound line cuts everything after that line, later turns included, and a gift whose turn was cut goes back into the inventory. A gift may be given over the unread lines too, and interjects exactly as words do; from a rewound line, a gift given past it does not count against the scene's one, that turn's cut handing it back. The row stands over the whole reply, its last line included, until that line is turned; Go from the action box opened over the last line reaches the decision point exactly as a click would, and sends the words as the turn. It is offered nowhere in a solo scene, the orientation scene's first reply, an exam or the ending's status lines, and under a cover; a class scene's first turn locks it instead. A rewind moves back one already-read line, through earlier replies as far as the scene's first reply, never onto the reader's own line, and changes only what is shown; it is closed while a reply's first line has not yet shown; the lines it stepped back may be read forward again, each landing as a cut, the reader's own line passed over without being shown, never into a line not yet read; the decision point is re-reached that way only when the rewind was taken from it. Any reply line of the scene may be rewritten from the chat log — never the reader's own, a status line or the landing's narration before his first action: the rewrite lands on the log, on the transcript line it was delivered as, on the box when it is the line on screen and on the decision point held in memory, so leaving carries it; a rewrite of a line the running summary already covers drops the summary marks past it, so the next call carries the line verbatim; it writes nothing itself. While the scene's ending is under way a line may still be rewritten for as long as the ending can be interrupted, behind a warning Settings can turn off: the ending's banked save and bookkeeping keep the original words, and the summary marks are kept too, dropped only if the player then interjects — so a finished ending files the whole recap and an interjection carries the rewrite verbatim. Interjecting on a reply that ended the scene, or on the goodbye lines after it, warns first and then discards the ending's bookkeeping for the later ending to redo, the texting ledger excepted, which the next ending claims.

**Casting.** The classifier only names; the app decides who is in the room. Every refusal is composed locally — not enrolled, no job, nobody named is free, a name he has not learned. An unnamed public action draws from the free pool, favouring the unmet; a passerby may walk in; whoever is standing at the scene's location joins; the worker on shift is there whether or not named. A plan outranks her timetable: only spring break takes an attendee off one, one with class or a shift in its slot still texts the reminder, saying she will meet him before or after it, and the plan's own two buttons — her reminder's and the landing's — cast every attendee he can reach who is in the city, whatever the words named, onto a scene whose opening says she has it after or has just come from it. A hangout — either of a plan's buttons, a meet-up agreed by text, an invitation he accepts — is also whatever kind of scene its words are, and is never refused for it: his class this slot, its students joining the people he is meeting and whoever does not take it sitting in; his shift, beside his coworkers; or a session on a project. Words the slot cannot hold — no class or shift of his now, a class not his, a lecture sitting its midterm or final, which he then skips, no project to work on — play as the meeting alone. Nobody is busy in the class or the shift the scene is, nor told she has it after. The model picks the order in each call, with nothing holding the two to agree; a typed action naming her is refused as busy. The cast is fixed for the whole scene. A `hide:` starts a departure clock, and after a grace period off the stage she has left and the model cannot write her back. A CG is offered only while the stage as written holds one girl with that CG on it — her stock eight whole, or one of her offered pairs — or nobody yet, and it ends the moment anybody else is shown or she herself leaves, by the scene or by the player's hand. The player may also put any whole CG of anybody in the scene over the whole stage from the Cast modal, every sprite under it, until he takes it off, the scene ends or she leaves it: one at a time, kept on the scene as a wardrobe lock is, never told to the model, and withheld under `noNsfwImages`; the stage, its sound and a save's picture all read the CG on screen through one rule.

**Relationships are memory, not score.** Nothing about how she feels is a number on disk. Each memory is typed (loved, liked, disliked, hated) and dated, and every one is kept; affection is derived from the most recent of them with a recency weight and bucketed into disposition tiers, which pick the behavior string she is written with. The prompts say how she feels with one phrase per tier, and the neutral, friendly and trusted tiers say something else in their upper half, neutral's counted from where everybody starts: rising the first time she is in it, a steady level once she has been out of it, above or below, a half she jumped over counting as one she has been in; which halves she has been in is kept on her save entry, read off her affection at every boundary once the clock has moved. A lover's neutral and friendly tiers read plain beside what the status line says of them. A cast scene's prompt carries only each girl's newest few, how many set per cast size in Advanced Settings, so changing that number erases nothing. A memory is kept and prompted in the third person, "the reader" every time: the ledgers are asked for it that way, what they return is filed that way, one the player types is filed that way, and every memory the app writes itself is worded that way. Wherever the player reads one it is turned into the second person with the verb agreeing; an older save's second-person memory is shown as it is and turned into the third person for the prompts. From her contact page he may rewrite a memory's verb and words, never its date, and the rewrite reaches every copy she holds. From a contact's page the player may also keep free notes on her: they are the save's, not the character's, and close her entry, as typed, in every prompt that describes her in full. Milestones — met, kissed, slept together, lovers, broke up, friendzoned either way, sharing, contact info, blocked — are flags the ledger's *events* transition, except the two intimate ones, which it reports as acts and the app derives from them; the model is asked for events, never for flags. A crush is rolled once, at the scene ending, gated on the reader meeting her preferred stat. A crush and a relationship are told as new until she has shared enough scenes with him: a count of scenes kept on her save entry, restarted at the boundary that gave her the crush or made them lovers — that scene, or those texts, not counting — raised at every boundary of a scene she was cast in, and gone once it settles or she holds neither; a save without one reads as settled, and the ending steps it in its projection as the boundary will. What her contact page reveals opens by tier and never closes again.

**The reader's stats and money.** Brain, Body and Heart are points on the save, spoken of and shown only as tiers. Every default is `Unremarkable`, at zero; New Game's full flow may start a stat one tier under it, below zero, a floor no stat ever falls through, and nothing in play lowers a stat into it. A stat moves only for something that happened in a scene: what the ledger says the hour exercised (more of it alone than with company), attending class, a grade coming back. Money is not a stat: a dollar balance the ledger prices each scene against, gifts spend and a job pays. It can go negative, and past the debt floor it ends the playthrough. The model is never shown the balance. The reader also has a bio: a few sentences the player writes at New Game (the full flow only) and may rewrite on his profile, carried into every prompt\'s `READER` block as one sentence that completes "The reader is". The save keeps lifetime tallies — money earned, kisses, nights, shifts worked — moved only by the same idempotent boundary passes that move everything else, and beside them the output tokens the writer has generated for this playthrough, thinking included, credited on every cloud text reply as it lands (retries and failed replies too, New Game's own generation calls carried over from the enrollment, and never a picture), so a replayed call is counted again because it was billed again; his GPA is never stored, being derived at read time from the earlier semesters' grade points on his transcript and this one's scores frozen and meetings recorded so far, standing at 4.0 before any exists; the aced exams and attendance his profile and his reputation count add the transcript's to this semester's.

**Academics.** The player picks lecture classes and a PE class, between a floor and a cap, from a catalog generated for the semester; every roster schedule is dealt locally. The generated semester is written to disk the moment the registrar offers it, so a playthrough can be continued from the registrar before its first save exists. Lecture classes are assessed by a midterm and a final built out of the factoids the ledger recorded week by week — a skipped meeting is a question answered wrong. Project classes are assessed at two showcases by sessions worked. A grade is the share his stats buy plus what his work buys of the rest, less what a Brain below zero owes, frozen when the assessment is sat. Add/drop day lets the timetable change once; a drop is permanent.

**Jobs.** The reader may hold one part-time job from a board, on a shift week that can never overlap a class. A shift is worked by spending the slot on it; a missed one is a strike, and enough of them and he is let go; clean streaks raise the wage. What a worked shift pays in stats is the employer's own, rolled when the shift is cast and kept on the scene, so a reload pays the same one. Roster characters hold jobs too, as whereabouts only: she is never judged or paid. The reader's job ends with the semester. An employer he quit or whose term ran out takes him back at the raises he left with and a clean record, with a welcome back; one he quit stays closed for the rest of that semester, and one that let him go never reopens.

**Whereabouts.** Every character has a hidden weekly schedule of haunts, dealt at New Game after classes and shifts, one person per place per slot. Each haunt comes from one of the profile's menus — somewhere she has fun, somewhere she studies, an activity the profile call describes in her own phrase, a meal out — and beside them the weekly grocery run and the nights she stays in. The kind is stored beside the place: it is what the prompts say she is there for, and what the rule emptying a study haunt through a week nobody has anything to study for reads. A place may keep hours of its own — a club's nights, a restaurant's dinners and weekend brunches — and neither a haunt nor an outing is placed outside them; an employer's shifts are its building's hours. Each slot also rolls an overlay of who is with whom — friends pulled along, weekend outings — off the roster's own affinities: a stored affinity per pair, moved a step by every meeting the slot held and settled at every scene ending. The map shows only what the reader has learned: contacts are told outright, anybody else only on a slot he has already found her on. Below five places it tops itself up with open places he can place nobody at, each under one `?` that stands for nobody in particular: places the casting would find somebody at come first, in an order fixed for the slot, and a Go there is any other Go.

**Rumors.** The ledger reports every kiss and every night as an *act*: who was in it — two or more only when they were all in it at once, which makes each of them a sharer — and whether anybody not taking part could have seen it. A girl in an act the reader is not dating is its subject; a lover is nobody's business. A public act is seen by everybody else in the room who was not in it, or, with nobody there, by one girl with nowhere to be; a private one leaves those same girls only a sign. What a girl holds about the reader and another girl is one dated suspicion, refreshed by every sign she gets and lapsing after the last of them. A girl who watched it, or who gets a second sign about the same girl while the first still stands, tells her friends and whoever she spent the slot with, once; being told is itself a sign. Lovers, crushes and the friends of a lover he has just cheated on take it personally the moment they see or hear it, as a permanent memory worded by which of the two it was; a girl who has agreed to share minds nothing, and nobody in the act ever witnesses it. None of it is read out at the scene's end: he learns what she saw or heard the next time he meets or texts her, since her memories reach every prompt. Dating is public: the reader's own block says who he is with and since when, an open relationship as such, and his exes with the dates theirs ran between. Starting with somebody new while a lover who has not agreed to share is still his leaves her that moment — she is left without a word said to him, she remembers being left for the other girl, and the opening writes the texts she ends it with — while a new lover beside one who shares is sharing too, and the girls with a crush of their own and the friends of the girl he left mind it as well; the milestone screens report only what the scene itself reached, never the dating pass. BunnyBot texts once the first time anybody sees him with a girl, and once with the two-timing tip.

**The Bunnyboard.** The phone: threads answered by their own texting call and released at typing speed, a second classifier judging each exchange for a meet-up that arms a scene, and an invitation he never answers keeping her thread out of that slot's texting ledger and costing him one authored line at the ending; friend requests that settle at slot boundaries; a feed, which weaves in location check-ins drawn per slot; a page per character. A contact may text an invitation of her own at a slot boundary — an acquaintance now and then, and more readily when she has no friends of her own — capped per slot and followed by a spell of quiet, and a run of invitations he does not take makes her wait longer each time; answering her by text costs nothing, though a lover minds being turned down, while a bare No or silence still costs. A contact who is more than an acquaintance may also reach out to him at a slot boundary just to talk, never to ask him out, about what the app picks for her — checking in on him, good news of hers, bad news of hers or a question: one girl a slot at most and never two slots running, none already texting him that slot about a plan, an invitation, a breakup or her leaving for the break, and none in class or at work, though a girl away for spring break may; she waits for her thread to go quiet before she does it again, longer for each reach-out he leaves unanswered, which costs him nothing else and keeps her thread out of that slot's texting ledger. On a slot an occasion he could go to is on and he is free — no class, shift or plan — every invitation that slot asks him to it, and when the roll picked nobody one contact is picked to ask, those the slot places nowhere first; an occasion he turned down or let stand is never asked about again, and Valentine's Day and the occasions nobody can go to prompt nothing. An invitation about neither a plan nor an occasion may be rolled into one of a fixed run of asks, tried in order until one lands and an ask she does not qualify for skipped: her room, for a Promiscuous girl on one of her lustful days who has slept with him and then for a lover or a friend with benefits; studying together for a midterm or final a few days off in a lecture they both take; his room, for a friend, a crush or a lover; a place the map shows that is open, none of her haunts nor her workplace, never open-air under rain, the hotel only for a lover or a friend with benefits; and four everyday asks — a favour, exercising together, a meal she cooks him, shopping — none asked again until all four have been, read off the invitations that carried them with no new counter. A plan made with somebody puts her on his contacts, so the calendar never names somebody the phone cannot reach, and every prompt that describes her in full, lorebook paragraph included, lists the plans she shares with him that are still ahead: a scene from the next slot on, a thread from its own slot, since texting comes before the scene; the slot opening carries only the plan she is reminding him of. Three contacts are not characters — VenusBot, BunnyBot, who hands the player the app's features as he earns them, and the boss — and none of them ever calls the LLM. A Profile tab is the reader's own page: his picture, the description the prompts carry of him, his bio and his tallies; it never repeats the landing's balance or stat tiers.

**Photos.** The phone's last tab is the playthrough's camera roll: pictures the cloud image model draws of the contacts the player picks, each in a wardrobe of hers that is whole on disk — the nude one withheld under `noNsfwImages` — and in one of her seven faces, from words he writes over a brief the form opens on, and optionally set in a background he picks: any place the scene may name or a roster girl's room, by day or by night, and in the rain only where the place has that render. Each girl reaches the model as her own reference picture, trimmed onto black as the graduation picture's lineup sheet is, unless there are more girls than the model takes pictures — a background counting as one of them — when they go as one such sheet; the background follows them as one more picture, so a model that takes a single picture takes no background. His words are sent as he wrote them, and under them the app adds a line naming the girls in the order their pictures stand, the sheet's left to right, and another saying the last picture is the background. A girl whose chosen sprite will not read, or a background whose picture will not, stops the photo before anything is sent rather than being left out. The model — Gemini's flash, flash lite or pro image model under Gemini; under a custom endpoint the images model it names first, then Gemini's own image models on Google's host or every model the images URL lists that takes a reference picture — and its size, ratio, thinking level and quality are offered only as far as the model takes them, the thinking level at the model's highest until the player lowers it. A new photo opens with nobody in it and the brief, drawn the way the playthrough's newest photo was; Edit and Retake open the form on that photo's own cast, background, words and terms. A photo is kept beside the playthrough with the in-game date and everything it was made from, so every save of the playthrough sees it, and Retake opens the form on it to make another beside it. Several may be drawn at once, each watched or sent to the background; one still being drawn finishes on the menu or under another save, and is lost to the app closing, to its playthrough being deleted, or to a backup being restored. A photo that failed is said only where nothing is moving under it — the landing holding the turn with an empty well, the Photos tab itself, the idle main menu — and waits anywhere else; its Retry re-sends the request verbatim, or writes the picture again where only the write failed.

**Gifts.** Five shops, an inventory, one gift a scene, to anyone standing on the stage, named or not: the reader's line calls a girl whose name he has not learned "her", since a turn saying a stranger's name is refused, and the prompt's note of the gift names her so the writer knows which girl it was. How it lands is the app's verdict from her gift preferences (a materialist reads the price first; a repeat is unimpressed), stamped at gift time so the ledger cannot price it twice.

**The ending.** Graduation morning plays an authored scroll and asks once for a status update from every Bunnyboard contact, dated into the week after graduation and filed on her feed when it lands; whether it has been asked is read off those dates, so a reload without them asks again. The curtain then falls into the epilogue's own night — every goodbye, cover and reload wears it — and a menu of goodbyes, lowest affection first: one scene each, no bookkeeping, no clock, and Go home last. The phone and the shop stay open and texting is closed; the phone's live rows (where she is now) are not shown. The log holds the ceremony, or the last goodbye. A reader who buys himself past the debt floor meets the collectors on whichever button he presses next; nothing is written between the purchase and the press, so the goodbyes save stands. The last picture is generated from a reference sheet of everybody he got close to and shown only when he goes home; the collectors close without it. The modal that follows offers the picture as a file — the native dialog on the desktop, the download panel in the browser — and its button is dead where none was drawn; beside it, Continue into the next semester, dead until the posts have landed, since the next semester carries them, and absent after his own graduation. Every way it and the posts can fail is silent.

**The Scene Creator.** One cast scene played outside any playthrough, from a setup the player writes: one to three girls, each with the wardrobe she opens in, a disposition, milestones, how she stands with the other girls and free notes; the reader's name, stat tiers and bio, read once a session off the newest save; a day between the first day and graduation, its half, its sky, an optional background and the opening action. The game runs it as a stay with no playthrough, so nothing is written but the saved scene: the store holds what the setup projects — her flags as the milestones would have left them, her affection one fixed figure inside her disposition's tier, the pairs at their zones, a sky wet in that slot alone, one of every gift — and the opening is the landing's first call to the cast the setup settled, never the classifier. The milestones are kept from contradicting each other by one rule, the game's own transitions read backwards: lovers rules out a crush and either friendzone, sex is a kiss too, a crush cannot stand below neutral, the two friendzones are one answer or the other, sharing holds only beside a lover, a crush or a friend with benefits and always for a Promiscuous girl, and a girl not yet met holds nothing and stands at neutral. Her picked wardrobe, a custom one without instructions included, is told in the opening's NOW block alone; there is no mood line and no line holding romance back on his stats; the fixed calendar's occasions reach it by date. The Bunnyboard and the calendar are not there, and the ending is the goodbye and nothing else: the ledgers, the boundary and the memory question give way to a question asking whether to keep the scene, also asked before leaving or quitting one part-way. A saved scene keeps the setup and every line the scene delivered, and lives in its own folder, install-wide; it replays as one reply queued from its first line, and interjecting at any line rebuilds the prompt from the setup with the whole transcript to the cut verbatim. A replay taken somewhere its lines did not go — a turn or a rewritten line — is saved as a new scene, and one read through unchanged asks nothing. A saved scene whose girl has since been deleted cannot be replayed.

**Replays.** A finished scene may be kept for the calendar to play again: the memory question carries a box, on by default, that keeps it; a boundary that asks nothing, with nobody cast in a solo scene or an exam, keeps nothing, and a boundary that loses the game writes no save and so no replay. What is kept is the scene as delivered — the reader's actions and every line of every reply, the status lines and the slot's opening narration not among them — with the cast and the charKey each of them was played under, so a girl renamed since is still the one who speaks. One replay is kept per half, and a day already played offers each of its halves' replays under that half's summary, to play or to delete; an earlier semester's only to play, under its own calendar and sky, and only while everybody in it is still in the game. A replay plays on the stage as one reply queued from its first line, the reader's own lines passed over unshown, and nothing in it can be interrupted, given, staged by hand or saved: the row, the Gift, the stage chips, the phone, the calendar and the Game menu's Save, Load and Quit are gone, and stepping back and forward, the chat log without its pencils or his lines, and hiding the chrome stay. The game it was opened from is held in memory exactly as a manual save would record it and loaded back from there at the replay's last line or its End replay, writing nothing either way; a replay is offered only where a manual save could be taken, and waits besides while any call is out that leaving would cancel and a load pay for again. Deleting a replay deletes it from every save of the playthrough at once.

**Sound.** Every sound is levelled in one mix file and nowhere else. The player's whole control over it is one slider per group; the first run's "No NSFW sound" box is no setting of its own, only the NSFW slider put at 0. The interface's own sounds fire from where the interface is, never from the loop. Nothing is ever said by sound alone. A text rings only while the phone is on screen; what lands under the curtain or during a slot's opening is rung for once by the landing, a landing reached by loading a save rings once for whatever is unread, and the boundary's own texts (a raise, BunnyMap's handover) land under the curtain.

Where a thing goes:

- Anything both processes read, every vocabulary, and every rule the desktop and the browser must both keep goes in `src/shared`; the disk edge and the database edge each call it. Every word the app says about a figure lives beside that figure's arithmetic, so retuning one reaches every reader.
- A prompt builder is pure and lives in `prompts/`; what sends it lives in `main`.
- A screen is one file in `views/` with one stylesheet in `vu_styles/`; a control is promoted to `components/` on its second caller; a mark a second screen needs moves into a `views/*Icons.tsx` file.
- Loop logic goes in `stores/loop/` beside the concern it belongs to, the pure half in its own file with `rand` injectable and the impure edge beside it.
- Runtime data lives only under the folder `paths.ts` roots; nothing else hardcodes a location.

## 4. Architecture rules

- **The renderer touches nothing but the bridge.** On the desktop, main is behind it and is the only process that touches disk, spawns a process or calls a network; in the browser build, `src/web` implements the same bridge over IndexedDB and `fetch`, and beyond `platform` the renderer cannot tell which it is talking to.
- **The window shows one page and opens no other.** A navigation the renderer starts is refused; a link leaves through main and only for an `https:` page or a `mailto:` address; the renderer is sandboxed, with devtools in dev only. In the browser build a wheel over the stage never scrolls the page the game is embedded in unless a scroller under the pointer can still take it.
- **Every channel is `invoke`/`handle` and answers `Result<T>`** — `{ ok, data }` or `{ ok: false, error }` — with errors built only by `appError`. Nothing throws across the bridge and nothing is swallowed. Adding a channel edits `preload/api.d.ts`, `preload/index.ts` and `main/ipc.ts`.
- **Progress rides fixed broadcast channels**, never per-job channel names: the renderer learns a job id only after the events have fired.
- **Character images reach the screen through one URL resolver — `charimg://` on the desktop, a blob URL in the browser — and shipped backgrounds through the bundler.** The player's own backgrounds come with their URLs on their listing, `bgimg://` on the desktop and a blob URL the browser keeps steady for as long as the picture behind it stands. The URL always names the PNG; the shipped cast is the same image under `.webp`, every reader accepts either extension and every writer makes a PNG. Which images a character or a background of the player's has is read off its folder on the desktop and off the pack index or the database in the browser, never recorded; a background's record is written after its pictures and removed before them, so a folder without one is absent. A URL can only show an image; what needs pixels — the wardrobe repairs, the hand fix, the graduation sheet, the picture a save carries — reads bytes over the bridge.
- **Sound is bytes over IPC into one Web Audio graph**, with one bus per slider. What plays continuously is derived from store state by one pure function, and what just happened by a second, with a reloaded save counting as nothing having happened. A missing file warns once and is silence.
- **The job queue is serial and global.** Failures retry with backoff only for transport errors; there is exactly one permanent-vs-retryable list, in `shared/errors.ts`, and both processes read it. `cancelGroup` resolves only once the running job has settled; loop calls register under three cancel groups — the scene calls, the ending's bookkeeping, and everything else — and leaving cancels all three, an interjection the first, an ending interrupt the first two. A photo is drawn beside the queue under a cancel group of its own, so it neither waits behind a render nor is stopped by leaving a game. No wait inside a job is unbounded; every continuation re-checks that its character is still wanted after each `await`.
- **Nothing in the UI waits for ComfyUI**: callers fire and forget or queue a job, and the first job pays the cold start. Main owns the server's runtime state and broadcasts it on one fixed channel; the renderer's store mirrors it and never infers it from a call's result. ComfyUI starts when the player asks for it in Manage Characters, after an install completes and when a render job needs it, and nowhere else — never at boot, and a game never uses it. Manage Characters can stop it by hand, and a stopped server stays down until the player starts it or a render job does.
- **One store per domain, no god store.** Only stores talk to the bridge; components read state and call actions; state one screen alone needs stays in React.
- **The game loop is imperative module code**, `gameLoop.ts` calling `stores/loop/*` with imports running one way and back-edges late-bound through `loop/hooks.ts`; there is no import cycle in `stores/`. Everything held between calls is one `loopState` object; `runToken` identifies one stay in a game and is re-checked after every `await`. At most one live scene call is ever in flight. The flags the screen must watch — a resolved scene's pending ending, whether an ending's bookkeeping is still out, the status sequence, the boundary's memory question — live on the store beside `busy`/`streaming`.
- **Errors have three tiers and none is silent**: a job failure marks its card and offers retry; a recoverable app error is a modal the app continues past; a fatal one is a blocking screen with copyable diagnostics.
- **Every console line lands in `data/app.log`** — the browser build keeps the same capped log in its database — from both processes: main writes its own as it prints, the renderer's are forwarded over one channel. A log that will not open costs nothing but the log. The Feedback modal hands the player a copy of it to send in, so nothing may print a secret. Both sinks — the file, and the error a failed channel answers with — replace the install, data and home paths with placeholders, so no line and no modal names the machine.
- **On disk, and in the browser's database**, every JSON record carries a `schemaVersion`; a read refuses a wrong version or a missing required field and defaults nothing. Two records carry a one-step upgrade, applied on read by a pure function beside the read rule and written straight back where it was read from: the character record, from the version before this build's, with `updatedAt` untouched; and the settings, whose custom endpoint's model ids move out of Gemini's fields when the file names none of the endpoint's own. The save and the enrollment read the version before this build's as this one, nothing in them having changed, and are written in this one only when next saved. Every other record, and every other version, is refused, with no migration path. Writes are atomic: a temp file renamed over the target, the rename retried for about a second when Windows refuses it because a scanner or the indexer holds the file it just saw change. A write that is several steps — a slot save pruning the window, a staged set committing — is one transaction in the browser. Any id that becomes a path segment is validated first. An absent optional is omitted, never `null`. What New Game settles goes on the playthrough record, written once and replacing the enrollment it was picked on, and rewritten after that only by a rename, which changes the player's name for the playthrough and nothing else, and by a later semester's Finalize, which adds that semester and touches nothing already there, an id the record already holds being refused; a name the record holds that cannot be kept is no name, never a refusal; what moves goes on the save. A save may carry its own picture inside it, so a backup and a restore carry it too.
- **The reader's picture is the one file under a playthrough the player supplies.** The renderer cuts it to the portrait's proportions and encodes a PNG; whichever build writes it sniffs the bytes first under one shared rule; it is kept beside the record like the graduation picture, goes with the playthrough when that is deleted, and travels in a backup, where the export sniffs it again.
- **A photo is drawn and kept in two steps.** The draw answers the picture's bytes and writes nothing; the renderer cuts the square thumbnail and keeps picture, thumbnail and record together, so a write that failed is retried without paying for the picture again, and a draw whose playthrough is gone is dropped unwritten. A photo write never creates its playthrough's folder or row, and deleting a playthrough or restoring a backup first stops every photo still being drawn and waits for it to settle. Which photos exist is read off the folder or the database; a missing thumbnail is cut again from the picture, and a backup carries the pictures and their records but no thumbnails.
- **A replay is kept once and named by the saves that can play it.** It sits beside the playthrough under an id that is the hash of its own record, so the same scene reached twice — a boundary crossed again after a reload, the shared days of two timelines — is one file, and a save carries only the ids it can play, by day and half. A replay is written in the same storage call as the slot save that first names it, one transaction in the browser, and is deleted the moment the last save naming it is deleted, pruned or rewritten without it: every write that removes or replaces a save compares what the old one named with what the new one names and deletes what no save left names, a delete taken while that playthrough is being played sparing what the running game will name in its next write. A replay an earlier semester's log on the record names is kept for the playthrough's life. A scan that has read every save's ids and the record's deletes any replay in the folder none of them names. Deleting the playthrough takes its replays; a backup carries those its saves or its record name, inside its record.
- **A save is written only at a point the player can be put back onto**, and every reply that arrived is paid for by a write before playback resumes. The registrar is such a point before any save exists, which is what the enrollment is for. A later semester's registration is self-contained, so the registrar reopens on it and the characters alone, and stands for the playthrough in the listings and on Continue while it is newer than every save; its Finalize writes the semester onto the record, then its first save into the boundary window, then removes it, one transaction in the browser. Leaving for the menu and quitting the app from the Escape menu both write the decision point first. Deleting the playthrough in progress, from the Game menu's Load Game, writes nothing: the game is left under the curtain and the playthrough deleted before the menu shows. The autosave is the last decision point reached and stands until the next one overwrites it; a boundary leaves it alone. An ending the boundary will lose to — the ledger's expulsion, or the balance past the floor once the scene's money has moved — is written nowhere: no autosave, and the manual save is refused over its goodbye and status lines, so a lost game leaves only the saves before it. An interjection writes the cut scene as a decision point before its call goes out; a rewind writes nothing. A scene ending is one write or none. A manual save is refused while a call it would lose is out — a reply, a classifier, an ending's bookkeeping, a slot opening, a text or its typed release, a failed turn parked on retry — and it is absent from the goodbye menu once the balance is past the debt floor. Where it can be taken, a manual save records the line on screen with the lines not yet read still queued, so a load lands on that line and reads on with no call; while the ending's status lines are on screen it records the ending's start, taken before any money moved, so a load replays the sequence and pays once; mid-paper it records the question the reader is on. The autosave for an exam is written once, when its questions land, and a reload sits the paper again from its first question; a manual save resumes at its own question instead. A manual save and the autosave carry a small picture of the stage a load of them opens on — for a reply still queued, as its first spoken line leaves it — drawn from the same images the stage shows and only when somebody, or a CG the player put there, is on it; the load list's background for a save without one is read off the same lines; a boundary save carries none, and the load list shows the landing's background for it. Every pass a boundary runs is idempotent, because the boundary is replayed whenever the save before it is loaded.
- A queued save write belongs to the stay that queued it and is dropped if that stay has been left; leaving, quitting, switching saves and deleting the playthrough in progress wait for every queued write.
- The load list is read as summaries, each save against its own semester of the record; a save is read whole only when it is loaded, and the newest save of a playthrough, whichever kind, is what Continue opens and what the playthrough list shows.
- **What a screen shows is derived where it can be**: the displayed stage, the calendar's classes, the map's rows and the feed's check-ins are computed at read time from facts, never stored beside them.
- **Booru tags are lowercase with underscores** in every vocabulary and prompt, normalized at the source; weight syntax is preserved as written; nothing the model returns is re-checked against a vocabulary except the pose, which cannot render without a skeleton.
- **The custom slots are outfit sets like any other.** Their ids are appended to the outfit vocabulary, so every gate, folder, URL, render, repair and lock takes them as it takes the stock sets; only what labels a set, reads its tags, offers it to RITA or sweeps for it tells the two apart. A record carries a seed flag only for a set it has rendered, and a set with no flag follows the main seed. The custom CG pairs are appended to the CG vocabulary the same way, two ids a slot, so every gate, file, URL, render, stage path and sound takes them as it takes the stock eight; only what labels a CG, reads its tags or its loops, counts the stock set as whole, offers it to RITA or sweeps for it tells them apart, and each pair is a set of its own for its seed and its staging.
- **A character's record has one writer in the renderer**, serialized per character: every write reads her fresh at its turn, so a seed a bucket records and a name the player types cannot lose each other, and a Save from the editor keeps the fields the run owns.
- **Configuration is the settings file**, edited through the app. Settings writes a select, a checkbox or a slider the moment it changes; the typed fields — the endpoint and images URLs, the model ids and the keys — are staged behind Save and gated on the way out. The Advanced Settings modal it opens writes each typed field when the field is left, a value it cannot take being held in the box and written nowhere: the reply cap and the sampling values, sent to a custom endpoint alone and not at all while blank, the memory budgets, and the player's own cast-scene persona. The three keys — the Gemini key, which writes and draws the cloud pictures under Gemini, a custom endpoint's own key, and its images key beside it — are encrypted at rest and never reach the renderer, which sees a presence flag for each. Switching providers moves no key and changes no other setting: each provider keeps its own model, secondary model and effort, so one switched away and back finds them as they were; a custom endpoint's key and its images key are each kept only for the origin they were typed for. The crossings are a blank images key's: it draws the pictures on the Gemini key at Google's own host where one is saved, the placeholder saying so, and otherwise on the endpoint's own key wherever the images URL points; the Gemini key is never sent to any other host. The images URL and model open on Google's root and the room model, and a save that leaves either at that default stores it as absent, so a later default reaches a player who never changed it. The images model is offered from the images URL's own list, asked for as its URL or key is left, and a list that arrived refuses a model it does not name, as the endpoint's list does its ids. A custom endpoint's URL is any well-formed `http:` or `https:` URL, saved as typed, the Test connection button being how the player learns whether it answers — the images one by drawing one real picture; a provider switched to Custom before its URL is typed is kept off a game by the writer-ready gate. The browser build is served over https, so a browser lets it reach `http:` only on this machine. The browser build holds the keys for the session only unless the player opts to keep them in the browser's storage — an origin every other itch.io game shares, and one the browser can clear. Either build writes a backup of everything it keeps — one zip in one format, never a key — and restores one behind a confirmation: saves, settings and grab bags are replaced and the player's own characters, backgrounds and saved scenes merged in, by id and by name, a background brought back whole over whatever is kept under its name, once every image in the archive has passed the same content check an import does. The backup's settings are checked as the stored ones are before anything is written, then merged in as a patch is, so the install keeps its keys, its dev and hand-edited switches and whether the browser remembers keys; both builds restore them through one rule. The shipped cast is the build's: it never travels in a backup, and a backup that names one of its ids is restored around her, record and images alike, so a change to the cast in one build is not written over by a backup made in another. A character's SillyTavern card and sprite pack are composed the same way in both builds, the renderer alone; each build only saves the bytes it is handed, desktop through the native dialog after sniffing them, the browser through the download panel, and neither file is anything the app itself imports. A few dev switches are hand-edited and have no UI; the browser build fixes them at their defaults, dropping any its stored row holds as it reads it.
- **The app comes up behind a cover already down.** Nothing is drawn until the stores are in and the first screen has been chosen under it; a fatal error drops the cover with the sequence it was raised for.
- **The first run is a sequence of stages on one screen** — the API key, the ComfyUI install, the content question — where a stage already settled is skipped and a player who quits part-way is put back at the first stage still unsettled. An update offer, where there is one, comes before the first stage. The key stage offers both providers — a Gemini key and model, or a custom endpoint's URL, model id and optional key with a Test connection, beside its images endpoint, optional key and model with a Test connection of their own — stages everything locally and writes nothing until Save; a custom endpoint's effort and secondary model wait in Settings, and its reply cap in Advanced Settings. The content question is last, and answering it is what ends the run. The browser build has no install stage, and a returning visitor who did not keep the key is put on the key stage alone.
- **Authored pools are dealt from grab bags**: the stranger's post, the inspiration word, the rumor place, the filler sentences. Half the pool drains before anything can repeat. The set-aside lists live in `data/grabbags.json`, shared by every playthrough, written behind the draw and flushed before the app quits. The stranger post's handle bag holds the generated names beside every supporter's, a supporter carrying as many marbles as their contribution earns on a diminishing scale, and the handle is drawn before the message: a marble cannot repeat before the bag turns, but a name with several of them can, and a line marked strangers-only is never dealt to a supporter's name.

## 5. Prompts and the LLM

- **Builders are pure** and return `{ system, user, schema, cacheKey }`; main attaches the key and sends. Arrays are joined with newlines, so one element is one line the model reads — no sentence is ever broken across two. A builder never draws from a grab bag: the caller draws the inspiration word or the rumor place and passes it in.
- **The player may replace the cast-scene persona.** A stored one heads every cast scene call verbatim, whatever `lessNsfwText` says, and reaches no other call; the slot opening, the graduation posts, the ledgers, the solo scene and texting keep their own. Saved identical to the shipped text, it is stored as absent.
- **Order is by rate of change, slowest first.** `system` is what never varies while a playthrough runs (persona, JSON rules, setting); `user` is everything else, cheapest-to-change last and the reader's action at the very end. Scene calls key their cache on the playthrough, never the save; one-shots key on a constant. A list that reaches a prompt is sorted, never shuffled, so the cached prefix holds. A `user` message that opens with a preamble fixed for its cacheKey marks where the preamble ends with `logFrom`, and the console log starts there; the system prompt is never logged.
- **The writer's window is the running summary plus the transcript's tail.** Every continuation and closing call sends the summary alongside the latest stretch of the transcript verbatim, taken as whole lines from the tail under a word budget and never shorter than the part the summary does not yet cover; the summary a reply arrives with covers the transcript up to the point that reply's prompt was built, and each summary is kept with the mark it covers up to, so a cut reverts to the right one. The ledger reads the whole scene.
- **Structured outputs everywhere**: the schema goes verbatim as `responseJsonSchema` to Gemini and as a non-strict `response_format` JSON schema to a custom endpoint; declared key order is load-bearing (`lines` before `summary`), and leaving a property out of the schema is the only reliable way to stop the model filling it. Gemini rejects a schema past a complexity cap with a bare `INVALID_ARGUMENT`, so a reply carrying a record per character is an array of records with a `key` enum and no item bounds. It also rejects an empty string as an `enum` value, so a field whose 'none' answer is `''` — the classifier's `classCode`, a scene line's `speaker`, a profile's job or destination — is declared a free string, not an enum.
- **Never trust a reply.** Every value is sanitized: an unknown key, background or action is dropped or defaulted with a console warning, never thrown on, because one bad field must not cost the player the scene. Streaming is a preview channel only; the resolved reply is sanitized whole and reconciled, and a preview that disagrees is discarded rather than spliced. The one carve-out is an interjection: lines the player has already read from a call he then interjects over are canon, and the call is aborted.
- **Prefetch, bank, apply.** The next slot's opening is fetched at the scene ending and banked on the save; a hangout scene is written the moment it is armed; the texting ledger is sent when the scene's cast is set and its reply banked on the scene, keyed on the message set it read, so a reload claims it rather than paying again; an exam's chain when its questions land. An interrupted ending's banked and in-flight bookkeeping is dropped and re-sent by the later ending, except the texting ledger, which the next ending claims. Nothing an ending reads is applied, only projected; the boundary applies.
- **A request is built once and re-sent verbatim.** A retryable code spends a silent backoff budget first, except a depleted prepaid balance, which a resend cannot refill; a content block earns one silent shortened resend; a malformed reply is never auto-retried once the player has read its preview. What survives is a modal, and leaving it loses nothing, because nothing was written; editing the prompt by hand is offered only on a content block, since nothing else that failed was the prompt.
- **Four kinds of call may route to a cheaper secondary model** — the slot opening, which the epilogue's status updates ride, texting, the exam quiz and the solo scene; cast scenes, ledgers, classifiers and character generation never do. Both classifiers run at no less than low thinking and both ledgers at high, whatever the setting says. A custom endpoint's effort is minimal until set, and the floors raise it like Gemini's.
- **Ask for events, not flags; for a boolean, not a magnitude.** The app holds every flag and applies every transition. The model is never shown the balance, never told the departure grace period, and never given a character's haunts.
- **The ledgers are asked for memories in the reader's voice** — "the reader", never "you" — and every desc is put through the same rewrite again as it is injected, so an older save's second-person memory reaches the model the same way.
- **The classifier decides nothing.** It names only the girls on campus, the app enforces, and every refusal is local; a transport failure parks the turn rather than degrading to a guess.
- **Every scene-kind fact is decided where the verdict is cast**, held on the store, persisted on the scene, and injected on every turn of it.
- **The lorebook is keyword-triggered** on the turn's own words and on the place the classifier named; entries never cascade. An uncast character who is named gets a paragraph that says she is absent, what she looks like, where she stands with the cast and the reader, and the plans she has with him still ahead; a graduate's says what she did and does instead of her year and dorm. Every class in the save's catalog is an entry too, saying when it meets, what it is and who takes it: its keys are derived from its code and title on every call and never stored, a word or phrase too many titles share keys nothing, and the class a scene is sitting in and the project a work session is for are left to their own blocks. A phrase pointing back at a finished slot — last night, yesterday morning, last Monday, Friday night, two days ago — brings that slot's summary out of the history, dated and marked as known only to those who were there; a phrase that lands on the slot being played or a later one brings nothing, and a text thread reads such phrases off its current slot's texts alone, an older text's having meant another slot.
- **A timetable names who sits beside him.** The reader's names, after each class, the classmates the calendar shows him there — one rule backs both — except in the class the scene is sitting in; a girl's names the other roster girls in each class of hers he does not take, his own line naming them where he does.
- **Authored text never meets the LLM or the sanitizer**: the first day, the bots, the game-over endings, the graduation scroll and every status line are the app's own writing.

## 6. Coding conventions

- TypeScript strict with `noImplicitAny` on explicitly. There is no linter; `npm run typecheck` and `npm test` are the automated checks.
- **Naming**: PascalCase only for React component and view files; camelCase for everything else. CSS classes are `vu-` prefixed and every visual rule lives in `vu_styles`.
- **Imports**: `@shared` is the only alias and is used for every cross-tree import of `src/shared`; imports inside a tree stay relative.
- **Comments say what the thing below them is or does.** One above every function, one or two lines, three where a mechanic genuinely needs it; a file's docblock at most five. Never what it isn't, never a rejected alternative, never history, and never a pointer to a guide or a section. A rule that needs explaining belongs in this guide, once.
- **Pure and impure halves are separate**: a roll takes `rand` as an argument, the impure edge reads and writes the store, and a test reaches the pure half.
- **A vocabulary is authoritative in code** and printed into the prompt and the schema from the same constant, so the two cannot drift; a new value is appended, never inserted, so cached prefixes stay byte-identical.
- **One definition per fact.** When two places must agree — how a charKey is derived, whether she is busy, whether a code is permanent, whether a schedule is complete, who is in a class — they call one function.
- **A control that is not the player's to use is absent; one that is dead says nothing.** A disable notice is an exception approved by name. Placeholders are real values a blank field submits, with one exception: an empty prose field of the character sheet shows the shape its text wants, told about her by name, and submits nothing.
- **A character whose name the reader has not learned is never shown by face or name** outside the scene she is in: showing the face is naming her, and a stranger is omitted rather than masked. The map's filler `?` is no mask: it is one per place, whoever is or is not there.
- **Nothing is shown as points.** A number on screen is a tier; the raw figure, over the next tier's threshold, appears in one place, small, beside the chart.

## 7. Tests

- The suite defends **logic that can silently corrupt a save, and the pure functions a save is computed from**. A loud failure is found the first time anyone plays; a plausible-but-wrong save is invisible for hours. IPC wiring, the ComfyUI and setup services, and every view stay untested by design.
- **Never test** prompt wording or block order, formatting, labels or copy, or shipped content — a lint of a hand-written file restates the file.
- **Err on the side of fewer tests.** Creating none for a feature and verifying it by hand is a valid outcome. Anything neither the type checker nor the suite can catch is exercised in the running app before it is called done.
- The environment is `node`: no test touches the DOM or React; the browser database is exercised under a fake in that same environment. Stores are exercised through `setState`/`getState`. `test/fixtures.ts` holds every builder more than one file wants, and `stubApi`, which installs a partial `window.api` — anything not stubbed throws. A builder that reads a store stays in its own file: `fixtures.ts` imports no store, or a suite that mocks a module beneath one would load that store from inside its own mock factory.
- One file per unit under test; the file names are the index, and each `describe` says what it defends.

## 8. External services

- **ComfyUI** is installed, verified and run by the app under `data/services/comfyui`: a runtime release, its custom nodes and its models, with `shared/setupManifest.ts` authoritative for every tag, commit, URL, size and hash. The runtime is one of two pinned assets of that release, NVIDIA or AMD ROCm, chosen by the GPU vendors Electron reports and overridden by the hand-edited `comfyGpu`; both are launched with the same arguments, ComfyUI reading its backend off the torch it was built against, and a build that is not the one the machine resolves to fails verification rather than falling back to the CPU, the next install replacing it and everything under it. On the ROCm build alone the background-removal node's source is patched to run its model on the CPU, at install and again at every boot that finds the patch missing; the NVIDIA build's file is never touched. Nothing is fetched from a moving reference — the runtime comes from a named release tag and a node from a named commit — and no download is accepted that is not over https or does not match the manifest's size and SHA256; a node's Python dependencies come from the package index only, at the exact versions the manifest names, since a player has neither git nor a build chain. Every zip the app opens is unpacked by the bundled 7-Zip and only after its whole listing has been read and accepted: no entry may be absolute, leave the destination, repeat a name or be a link, and an archive past its entry-count or uncompressed-size cap is refused unopened. A character import and a backup restore hold to the same rule once the zip is open: besides the record, the marker and a staged run's images, every entry must be one of her images under either extension with its bytes sniffed rather than trusted, or the whole archive is refused before anything is written, and the export, duplicate and backup writers copy only what that gate accepts, so the app never writes an archive it would refuse. The workflows are API-format graphs executed as they are, every prompt string injected per job. One seed per character; a regenerate renders into staging and commits by a directory swap, a fill writes in place. A single expression and a single CG render in place under a seed nobody records; a single expression is a face pass over the set's base frame, which is rendered only where the set has none. The app trusts only the server it owns: one already answering on the port is adopted only while a process is running from the app's own interpreter, an output path the server names is used only where it lands inside the output folder, and an image it answers is written only when its first bytes are a PNG's or a JPEG's.
- **The cloud LLM** is called by one adapter per wire format: Gemini's native `generateContent`, and OpenAI chat completions for the custom endpoint. The transport, the vendor table and the adapters are three files with three jobs; the table alone names a vendor for writing, and the custom entry's root is read off the settings at call time. The pictures have adapters of their own, one per wire format: under Gemini always Gemini's native call on the Gemini key and its two image models, pro for the graduation picture and the player's pick of three for a photo; under a custom endpoint the one its images URL speaks — Gemini's native call on Google's own host, OpenRouter's Images API anywhere else — on the images model for every picture but a photo, which may be drawn on any model the form offers. A custom endpoint's picture refused while it asked for a size is asked once more at the model's own. Every picture is landscape but a photo, which asks for the player's ratio, and for a size, a thinking level — Gemini's two flash image models alone take one — and a quality only where the model takes them; what a custom endpoint's model takes is read off the Images API's own model list, a host with no such list being offered a common set. Gemini's image finish reasons are read as a block or as no picture, never as a cut-off scene. A photo waits five minutes at most, and is re-sent silently only on a failure no vendor bills. The Settings form's four probes — the endpoint's model list, the images URL's model list (the Images API's own list of the models it accepts, or Google's models narrowed to its image models, Google being asked only with a key), a one-line test call and one test picture — go through the same transports. Every text call is capped at one shared output-token ceiling, whichever adapter sends it, except that a custom endpoint sends the player's own cap where one is set, and the player's sampling values beside it on every text call — `temperature` and `top_p` as chat completions names them, `top_k` and `repetition_penalty` as the extensions such servers read at the top level of the same body. Gemini is sent none. What every text reply generated, thinking included, is read off the vendor's usage block by the transport on every exit — a blocked, truncated or malformed reply included — and reported through a port each process wires, reaching the renderer on one fixed broadcast channel before the reply itself does; the pictures report nothing. A cloud image is kept exactly as the model answered it, JPEG or PNG, under its `.png` name; the night re-light is the one reader that needs the type and sniffs it off the bytes.
- **Updating.** The desktop asks itch.io's channel endpoint for the Windows build's user-version at every boot, offline answered with silence, and compares it to its own. A newer one is offered once per launch before any screen, the first run's stages included, unless the Settings checkbox has turned that offer off; either way the main menu carries a notice that offers it again, and an update taken from there runs under a curtain that lifts back onto the menu if it fails. On Update the build is fetched through the store page's own download handshake, unpacked under `data/update` — once whatever the last update left there has been swept — behind the same zip gate as every archive, and checked to be a newer build of this app by its manifest before anything moves. The swap runs after the app has quit, by the app's own exe in Node mode on a helper script it copied out, by renames only with a journal and rollback, and the app relaunches itself; `data/` is never touched. A swap that did not land leaves a note in `data/update`, which the next boot reads before sweeping the folder and says in a modal before anything else is asked. A file is removed only when the installed build's manifest listed it and the new build's does not, so an install without a manifest gets every file replaced and nothing removed. `resources/build-manifest.json` is written at packaging, once the exe's icon and version strings are in, and is what both sides are planned from. `updateAsVersion` and `updateFeed` are hand-edited dev switches.
- **Release.** `npm run release` builds both artifacts from one tree and one version: the browser folder and its zip (`index.html` at the root, every URL relative, asserted against itch.io's caps before it is zipped) and the Windows app folder and its zip (electron-builder's `zip` target, never `portable`; the shipped content beside the exe, and `data/` never inside; the folder is refused unless every file in it, and nothing else, is in its manifest at the size and hash it has). Shipped art is transcoded PNG → WebP through a content-addressed cache, so a re-run is byte-identical and butler's patches stay small; the desktop ships the cast as files, the browser as one stored zip per character in the export format. The version is `package.json`'s alone: `npm version` bumps and tags it, and the script refuses a dirty tree and a tag that points elsewhere. The push's `--userversion` is what the updater's check reads, so a build pushed without one is invisible to it.
- **Nothing is redistributed**: weights, runtime and nodes are fetched from their own projects at install. `CreditsModal` is the authoritative credit list, headed by the donors and playtesters read off `assets/supporters.json`, which `npm run supporters` derives from the ledger in `private/`; a new manifest entry or shipped sound is not done until it has a line there, and a new supporter is not done until the script has run and the derived file is committed.
- **Public mirror.** `scripts/syncPublic.mjs`, driven by `.github/workflows/sync-public.yml`, publishes a snapshot of this repository's `main` to https://github.com/venus-uni-dev/venus-university on every push, one squashed commit per push. `isPublic` in that script is the only rule deciding what goes public; a new folder under `assets/` is private until it is added to its allowlist there. `assets/characters` reaches the mirror as one stored zip per character, built from HEAD's blobs with a fixed entry date so an unchanged cast produces no commit, and the sync fails on a zip of 100 MiB or more, GitHub's file cap. `.github/`, `build/itch-page/`, `private/` and the two music folders `assets/sound/music` and `assets/sound/ambient_music` never ship; `assets/sound/README.md` lists where each excluded track comes from. The runner fetches the mirror with a blob filter, so the public art is never downloaded. A commit that touches the ledger is titled without a figure, since the mirror's squashed commit carries every private subject. The mirror is never merged from. The mirror is now live.

## 9. Keeping this guide

**Add**, in the same change:

- a rule or invariant the change relies on that the code cannot say for itself;
- a new mechanic, at one paragraph in section 2;
- a new folder, or a new kind of file, in the map;
- a convention the moment a second file follows it.

**Do not add**:

- anything readable off the code or a comment — schemas, signatures, channel lists, node ids, the words on a screen;
- tuned numbers, rates or thresholds;
- history, rationale essays or rejected alternatives;
- appearance, which is `UI_STYLE_GUIDE.md`'s.

Update a paragraph when a change makes it untrue and delete it when the thing it described is gone. Say each thing once, in whichever of the two guides owns it, and point at the other rather than restating it.
